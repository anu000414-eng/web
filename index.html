Introduction to Bharathapuzha

Bharathapuzha, popularly known as Nila, is one of the important rivers of Kerala. It is the second-longest river in the state and plays a significant role in the physical, economic and cultural landscape of central Kerala.


The river originates in the Anaimalai Hills of the Western Ghats and flows westwards through parts of Tamil Nadu and Kerala before joining the Arabian Sea near Ponnani in Malappuram district.


Bharathapuzha is an important source of water for agriculture, settlements and other human activities. The river basin also has considerable ecological, historical and cultural significance.

Geographical Information

The Bharathapuzha river basin extends across parts of Kerala and Tamil Nadu. Its landscape includes the Western Ghats, foothills, plains and coastal areas. The river is connected with several tributaries that contribute to its drainage system.

River Length

Approximately 209 km

Origin

Anaimalai Hills, Western Ghats

Outflow

Arabian Sea near Ponnani

River Basin Area

Approximately 6,186 sq. km

Direction of Flow

Westward

Major Region

Central Kerala and adjoining Tamil Nadu

Natural Resources of Bharathapuzha

Water Resources

Bharathapuzha and its tributaries provide water resources for irrigation, domestic use and other activities.

Agricultural Resources

The river basin supports agricultural activities. Irrigation facilities contribute to cultivation in different parts of the basin.

Forest Resources

The upper catchment is associated with the Western Ghats ecosystem, which supports diverse vegetation and wildlife.

Ecological Resources

River channels, wetlands, riparian vegetation and associated habitats support biodiversity and ecological processes.

Tourism Resources

The river basin contains scenic landscapes, cultural locations and places of recreational importance.

Cultural Resources

Nila has a strong connection with the literature, traditions, history and cultural identity of Kerala.
